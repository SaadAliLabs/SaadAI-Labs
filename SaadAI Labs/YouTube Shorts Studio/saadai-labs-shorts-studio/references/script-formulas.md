# Hooks, scripts and loops

YouTube shows every new Short to a small test audience and widens reach if they **watch instead of swiping**
("Viewed vs. swiped away" in Studio). Almost all of that decision happens in the first 1-3 seconds, then
retention to the end and replays (loops) decide how far it travels. Write for that.

## Hard rules
- **Frame 0 shows the payoff.** The first scene is a `hook` (or a `story` scene with a `title`) whose text *is* the
  claim. No logo, no "hey guys", no build-up, no "in this video".
- **Narration starts immediately** and the first sentence lands in ≤ 3 s (≤ 14 words).
- **Length:** 20-45 s is the sweet spot (≈ 55-120 words of narration at ~2.7 words/s). Never over 60 s.
- **A visual change every 1.5-4 s.** One idea per scene; split any scene that needs more than ~14 words.
- **One sentence of narration per scene** (two short ones max). The caption *is* the narration, so cut filler.
- **Pay off the hook fully.** A hook that overpromises loses viewers at second 5, which hurts more than a calm hook.
- **No engagement-bait outros** ("like and subscribe"). End on the loop line instead. A channel CTA belongs in
  the pinned comment, or as ≤ 4 words inside the last line at most.
- Plain, spoken English: short words, contractions, present tense, "you".

## 12 hook patterns (pick 3 per short, then choose the strongest)
1. **Impossible claim:** "This guy sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."
2. **You-test:** "Try tickling yourself right now. You can't."
3. **Hidden villain:** "Your phone is lying to you about battery percentage."
4. **Number shock:** "You blink 28,000 times a day, and miss 40 minutes of your life."
5. **Myth flip:** "Goldfish don't have 3-second memories. They remember for months."
6. **Forbidden / secret:** "Airlines don't want you to know this seat trick."
7. **Ranking tease:** "The 3 deadliest animals, and number 1 is tiny."
8. **What-if:** "What if the Moon disappeared tonight?"
9. **Mistake:** "You've been brushing your teeth wrong your whole life."
10. **Before/after:** "This used to take 3 hours. Now it takes 30 seconds."
11. **Story cold-open:** start mid-action: "He had 10 seconds to jump."
12. **Direct question with a twist:** "Why is the sky blue? It's not why you think."

Score each option 1-5 on: curiosity gap, clarity in 2 s, visual punch (can frame 0 *show* it?), honesty
(does the video really pay it off?). Pick the top total and show the user all three with scores.

## Format A: Explainer (facts, tips, how-it-works, AI/tech, finance concepts)
Six beats, ~35 s:
1. **Hook** (`hook`): the claim/payoff, 1 sentence.
2. **Setup** (`icon` or `text`): the one thing you need to know.
3. **Reveal 1** (`compare` / `stat` / `list`): the core mechanism, shown, not told.
4. **Reveal 2** (`stat` / `list` / `text`): a number or example that makes it real.
5. **Twist** (`text` / `icon`): the surprising implication ("It's also why…").
6. **Loop** (`end`): a line that flows straight back into the hook.

## Format B: Story (history, "true story", fables, mini-dramas, scenarios)
The cartoon-story formula behind the viral cartoon channels (≈ 45-65 s, 6-9 scenes):
1. **Impossible opening line** shown as the scene title ("He sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.").
2. **Escalating scenes:** each scene raises the stakes or adds a crazier detail.
3. **A laugh beat:** a reaction (shocked face, facepalm, a prop falling from the sky).
4. **The "You see…" line:** the one-sentence explanation of how/why it happened.
5. **Closing line that flips or repeats the opening** so it loops ("So he came back, and sold it again.").
Characters: 1-2 per scene, re-used across the channel (series characters build recognition). The speaker
gets `"speaking": true` so the mouth moves with the words.

## Format C: Myth vs fact
Hook = the myth stated confidently → `compare` (Myth vs Fact, winner right) → `stat` proof → `text` why the
myth exists → `end` loop.

## Format D: Ranking / top 3
Hook teases #1 → #3 (`icon`) → #2 (`icon`) → #1 (`stat` or `icon` + shake) → `end` that re-teases.
Count down, never up.

## The loop (replays push retention past 100%)
Write the last line so it grammatically flows into the first line:
- Last: "So go ahead." → First: "Try tickling yourself right now…"
- Last: "And that's why he came back and…" → First: "…sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."
Keep the last scene short (1.5-2.5 s) and visually close to scene 1.

## Writing the on-screen text
- `title`/`text`/`label` are **not** the narration: they are 2-7 punchy words that summarise the beat.
- Mark 1-2 words per line as `*accent*` (the yellow/pink highlight). Accent the surprising word, not filler.
- Numbers as digits on screen ("28,000"), words in narration are fine either way.
- Emojis are the illustration system: pick literal, instantly readable ones (🧠 🦈 💰 ⏱️ 🔥 🗼), one main emoji per scene.

## Writing for the voice (so it never sounds flat)
The narrator reads exactly what you write, so punctuation is the performance:
- Short sentences. One breath each. Break long ones with a full stop, not "and".
- Use `!` on genuine surprise and `?` on real questions; the voice lifts on both.
- A comma or `…` before the reveal word creates a dramatic pause: "And the reason… is kind of creepy."
- Contractions and casual words ("kinda", "here's the thing", "wait") sound human; "therefore", "additionally" don't.
- Spell numbers the way you'd say them when it matters for rhythm ("a hundred milliseconds").
- Vary sentence length: short, short, longer, short punch.
